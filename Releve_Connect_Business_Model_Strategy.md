## The shift (read this first)

**We are not selling subscriptions. We are building something people don't want to leave.**

The old frame was acquisition: tiers, pitches, "join and pay." The new frame is retention by value — *create enough that people want to stay,* and the staying takes care of itself. As Relevé grows and changes, so does the vision, and the vision has caught up to the truth: this was never a software sale. **It's about building relationships.** Every pilot, every invitation, every partner is a relationship first. Nobody is "pitched our software." They're welcomed into something worth belonging to.

Pricing still exists — it's what people choose to stay for once the value is real. But it never leads. We don't put a price on the first date.

## Why value-first is also the smart business

This isn't only the kind version — it's the durable one.

- **Retention beats acquisition economics.** A platform people stay in because it's woven into their week has near-zero churn and near-zero re-acquisition cost. You're not renting attention; you've become infrastructure.
- **Relationships are the moat.** Anyone can copy a feature list. No one can copy the trust of five founding schools who built it with you, or a college team that came in because you knew them. The relationship *is* the defensibility.
- **The lanes feed each other.** Comp schools (Lane 1) grow the families and future dancers; Bergen PAC (Lane 2) produces the content and hosts the events; college teams (Lane 3) become the professional roster the studios eventually hire from. One ecosystem, three relationships, all reinforcing.
- **Value-first still has to pay.** The soul is relationships; the discipline is that when a pilot chooses to continue, the ratified pricing is what they continue at. Both are true at once — earn the stay, then honor the terms.

## Demand-side revenue lanes — colleges & industry pay Relevé
Restated principle: **the demand side pays; talent never does.** Demand for Relevé's talent has two ends, and both are paying customers.

**Lane A — Colleges (recruitment / advertising → to Relevé).** Take the page from NYCDA and improve it. Lanteri earns from students' four mandatory convention days, the colleges show up and award millions in scholarships, and NYCDA *claims the impact* ("$26M awarded"). **Keep the claim — "Relevé connected students to $X in scholarships" is marketing gold that costs us nothing — but invert who pays: colleges pay Relevé to recruit; families don't.** Colleges have real enrollment-marketing budgets and want exactly our audience: serious, pre-qualified, college-bound performers.

**The advertiser money is Relevé's — not bergenPAC's.** Kathleen's call, and the structurally correct one: a 501(c)(3) running for-profit ad revenue creates UBIT tax problems, so clean separation protects *both* parties. **Relevé** owns the college contracts and keeps the revenue; **bergenPAC** provides venue, mission, and credibility, and shares the scholarship-impact halo. Scholarship dollars flow college → student directly; Relevé claims the impact.

*How a college pays Relevé:* (1) **Partner membership** (annual, verified presence + directory) · (2) **Forum recruitment access** (per event — *the early money, before platform scale*) · (3) **Featured placement** (targeted spotlights by discipline/year/region) · (4) **Sponsored programming** (colleges fund the panels/master classes families value) · (5) **Opt-in recruitment connections** (consent-based, guardian-approved).

**Lane B — Industry / Broadway (→ to Relevé).** The demand side isn't only colleges. Casting, agencies, and producers pay to reach the *professional* roster the same way colleges pay to reach students. **Bruce Robert Harris — on the board, the bridge to Broadway (from Kathleen's years directing the Elan Awards master-class series with him) — is the on-ramp and the credibility that opens this lane.** Colleges recruit the college-bound; industry recruits the professionals; Relevé monetizes access to its talent at *both* ends of the pipeline.

## Sequencing (so this doesn't get ahead of itself)
Audience and relationships come first; **advertiser revenue scales with the audience.** Don't sell college or industry advertising before there's a roster and a following to offer. The pilot (studios, families, the college team, bergenPAC) builds the audience; the **Forum is the early wedge for college money**; the larger platform-advertising and industry revenue follow as the pipeline fills — accelerated by the music/theatre expansion.

## The education pillar — The Financial Floor (founding, not optional)
Relevé's education side has a founding pillar: **financial literacy for dance professionals,
present and future.** Dancers stay poor because no one teaches the money *and* because a
dancer's year is structurally built to keep them broke — seasonal income (studios run
Sept–June), unpaid vacations and holidays, inconsistent and rarely steady work. Relevé teaches
a dancer **to see their own year and plan it** — layered by age, delivered by the same
platform from a guardian-managed child to a working professional (the age-18 conversion is the
spine). It rides on foundations already built (the calendar knows the season, the roster knows
identity, Swing/Flex know the income, dashboards shape by role) — an extension, not a rebuild.
It obeys the payer rule: **sponsored financial literacy** (financial-wellness orgs, credit
unions) funds the teaching so **talent gets it free.** Seed it from day one via **Live Pass
events, The Climb, and live Zooms** aimed squarely at it during launch. Education, never
personalized advice; minors inside the guardian-consent container.
